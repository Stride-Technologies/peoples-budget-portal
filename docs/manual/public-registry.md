# Public Registry — Citizen's Guide

The **public registry** is the part of the portal anyone can use **without
signing in**. It lets residents browse the city's budget and send feedback on it.

> No account is needed. Everything in this guide works from a public browser.

## Where to start

The public site is organised around the difference between a **plan** and a
**law**, because they are different documents and comparing them carelessly
misleads:

| | What it is | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual Investment Program** | What the city's offices *propose* to do next year. Filled in office by office over months, so it is incomplete while it is being written, and nothing may be spent on it. | `/aip` |
| **Annual Budget** | The same plan after the Sanggunian cut it to fit the money and passed it as an ordinance. Complete, settled, and it authorises spending. | `/annual` |

Each has two pages:

- **Overview** — a plain-language read of where the money goes: what it is aimed
  at, which offices hold it, whether it pays people or builds things, and which
  funds it draws on (shown only for a year whose budget records its funds; the
  FY2026 book records them only for its special funds). Start here.
- **Browse** — the full searchable table described in the rest of this guide.

A year's AIP pages carry a short note on where the plan stands — for 2027,
**Approved by the Sangguniang Panlungsod**. The first time you open them, a one-minute guide
explains what can still change, what the amounts mean and how to comment. Open it
again at any time with **How to read this page**.

An entry the city has replaced shows an **Archived** note when you open its
link. It is not part of any total, and new feedback on it is closed.

**Languages.** The home page, the Overviews and Learn can be read in English,
Filipino or Bikol — choose one at the top of the page. Budget terms (such as
*Appropriation* or *MOOE*) and the names of offices and programs stay in English,
exactly as the city's documents print them, so you can match what you read here
to the official record. Spot a clumsy translation? Choose **Suggest a better
translation** at the bottom of the page.

**Learn** walks through the budget cycle stage by stage: choose a stage
in the diagram to see what happens, who is involved, and where residents can take
part. Three more pages sit beside it: **AIP vs Annual Budget** (why the two
documents show different numbers), **Reading an entry** (programs, projects,
activities and the reference code) and the **Glossary** (every term the site uses).

The combined table at `/budget` still works and still shows every year in one
place; it is no longer in the menu because the two sections cover it more
clearly.

## What you can see

The registry shows programs that have reached the **public stage** — that is,
proposals that are awaiting final committee approval or have already been
**approved**. Earlier drafts and proposals still in internal review are **not**
shown; a proposal only appears once it is far enough along the workflow to be
public.

For each program you can open:

- **Overview** — the proposing office or organisation, its strategic finish
  line, sector, expected output, and the budget breakdown (Personnel Services /
  MOOE / Capital Outlay) with the grand total.
- **Projects & activities** — each project or activity under the program, with
  its own budget and line items.
- **Version history** — every published version of the program and when it was
  published. Each version is labelled with what changed (for example "Estimated
  Cost −₱15,390,000.00", "Moved to CDRRMO", "4 amounts changed"). Open a version to
  see **What changed since** the one before: the amount before and after, office,
  fund or finish-line changes, each activity whose amount changed, and what was
  added or removed — with **Show items** for a whole project.

## Browsing and filtering

Pick an **AIP cycle** (fiscal year), then narrow the list by:

- **Cluster** and **city office**
- **Finish line** (one or more)
- **PAP sector**
- **PAP class**: General Administrative Services, Support to Operations or
  Operations. An entry with no class counts as Operations, as on the printed form.
- **Fund source**: one or more funds. An entry matches when it, or any of its
  projects and activities, is paid from that fund. **Not recorded** finds
  entries with no fund at all.
- a free-text **search** over the program name or its AIP reference code

## Special funds

Some money is set aside by law for a purpose: the **Special Purpose
Appropriation (SPA)**, the **20% Local Development Fund (LDF)** and the **5%
Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Fund (LDRRMF)**. Each entry paid
from one is listed under the office that runs it, with a small **SPA**, **LDF**
or **LDRRMF** label beside its code. Hover over the label for the full name.

Each special fund also has a page of its own in the Annual Budget, for example
`/annual/2026/funds/ldf`. Reach it from the links in the Overview's "where it
comes from" section. The page shows:

- how much of the year's budget the fund holds;
- what the law says about it, and which law;
- every entry, grouped by office.

The law sets a minimum for some funds, for example at least 20% of the city's
National Tax Allotment for the LDF. The page cannot check that minimum yet,
because the figure it is a share of is not on the site.

You can sort by most recently updated, newest, highest budget, or most
feedback. The list is paginated and shows the total budget of the programs that
match your filters.

## Income and spending

When a year's budget book carries them, the Annual Budget has two more tabs:

- **Income** ("Where the money comes from") is the Receipts Program. It has the total available for the year, its sources (taxes, fees, the National Tax Allotment, the city's enterprises) and every line as the book prints it, beside the two years before. "One-off" marks income the city does not expect every year. The hospital, market, abattoir, bus terminal and college each keep their own receipts too. Those are shown apart, because their income is already counted in the city's total.
- **Spending** ("What each office spends on") lists every office. Pick one to see its budget by object of expenditure (salaries, supplies, equipment) for the last two years and this one. Below that are the city's loans (LBP Form 5), its obligations set by law or contract (LBP Form 6), and the legal cap on salaries.

A year that has neither shows Browse only.

## Sending feedback

You can leave feedback anonymously — no account required:

- **On a specific program or project/activity** — open it and use its feedback
  form. Your message is attached to that item.
- **General feedback about the registry** — use the general feedback option
  (not tied to any one program).

Give your **name** (and optionally an email) with your **message**. To prevent
abuse, feedback is **rate-limited** (a handful of submissions per 10 minutes);
if you hit the limit, wait a few minutes and try again.

Submitted feedback is **reviewed by portal administrators**. It is not published
back to other visitors as a public comment thread, and it does not change a
program's status on its own — it is a channel for the city to hear from
residents.
